# Consequence Loop Examples

The "delayed consequence" loop is the second proof clip on the landing page. It says: what you do comes back. Sometimes you see it coming. Sometimes you don't.

These are the documented examples from the operator's transcripts and play sessions. Lift as quoted material; do not embellish.

## The Two Anchor Examples

### The king-of-England nukes Canada

The operator's verbatim description:

> "Or you can have the case where the king of england randomly gives you nukes to use on Canada for no apparent reason. And President Tuna Fish of the great white north endorses the whole thing, only to attack you using the nukes as pretext after you use them in the way the UN bafflingly permits you to."

The shape:
1. Player receives an absurd gift (King of England gives nukes).
2. Player uses the gift in a way the UN bafflingly permits.
3. The recipient's leader (President Tuna Fish) endorses the use.
4. President Tuna Fish then uses the precedent as pretext to attack the player.

The lesson: an action the player treated as a joke becomes the legal/diplomatic pretext for a later attack. The world remembered the gift, the endorsement, and the permission, and weaponized them in combination.

### The revenge of the deposed leader

The operator's verbatim description:

> "Well often you destroy some nation. And end up making an enemy of a former leader or some person who lived there who comes after you at a later point."

The shape:
1. Player destroys a nation (conquest, capture, decapture).
2. A leader or notable figure from that nation becomes an NPC with a grudge.
3. The NPC returns at a later point with hostile intent, often with allies or resources they didn't have before.

The lesson: every conquest creates a nemesis. The karma system (per `autogenesis-game-mechanics` § Karma & Nemesis Spawn) supports this mechanically: 20 qualifying hostile actions fill the karma bar, the bar resets, a nemesis spawns. The operator's example is the narrative-level instance of the same mechanic.

## The Syrup Trap Anecdote

The operator's verbatim reference:

> "Making the right call at the right time, understanding narrative logic, and gameplay logic liike syrup traps sentient construction demolotion robots..."

Shape (inferred from context):
- Player uses syrup as a trap.
- The trap catches sentient construction demolition robots.
- Demolition robots are notable because they're one of the entities strong enough to demolish the sky (per the Robert the Destroyer example).

The lesson: the syrup trap is a working example of "narrative logic + gameplay logic" intersecting. The syrup is canonical to the Lord Maple Tree character; the robots are canonical to Robert the Destroyer. The player exploits the established world, not the rules.

This anecdote is a good *visual* proof clip if the trace shows it (the syrup-trap moment isn't in the four verified trace folders, but the operator's reference is durable). Use as an aside in the consequence-loop section, not as the lead example.

## The Linear-Causality Note

The operator's verbatim framing:

> "It's often fairly obvious because the story moves forward in a linear pattern even if the story is completely insanne and absurd."

The marketing implication:
- The "delayed consequence" isn't really delayed in the reader's perception. The story is linear. The reader (and the player) usually sees the cause-and-effect chain.
- The surprise comes from not realizing the earlier detail WAS the cause, not from the cause being hidden.
- This means the consequence-loop copy can promise "you'll usually see it coming" — that's not a contradiction with "sometimes you don't." Both are true. The world is linear; the reader's awareness is the variable.
