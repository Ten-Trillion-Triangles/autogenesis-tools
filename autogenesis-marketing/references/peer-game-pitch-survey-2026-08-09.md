**Purpose:** Verbatim quotes and patterns from 30+ landing pages surveyed for the Autogenesis premise-sentence revision. Load this when:

- Justifying a wording choice in the body copy (proof-clip section, hero sub-line, etc.).

**Surveyed:** 2026-08-09 against the operator's pushback on Variant 1. Three threads: PvP board games, AI-driven narrative games, roguelike / possibility-driven games.

## The four-promise standard (operator correction, 2026-08-09)

The operator's verbatim framing — *"the concept that you can do ANYTHING and it works and still fits in the rules of the game and feels like a game is important"* — articulates FOUR load-bearing promises, not three:

1. **ANYTHING** — unbounded player agency (you don't pick from a menu).
2. **WORKS** — the world produces a real response (not empty acknowledgment).
3. **FITS IN THE RULES** — the response is mechanical, rule-bound (validator/judge pipeline), not vibes.
4. **FEELS LIKE A GAME** — scoring, factions, win conditions are visible (51/55/60% victory thresholds, 25-round cap, the four-player competitive frame).

Premises that satisfy 1+2+3 but elide 4 collapse into "AI writes funny stories." Premises that satisfy 1+2 but elide 3+4 collapse into the "AI chatbot" anti-pattern. Premises that satisfy 1+4 but elide 2+3 (Variant 1) collapse into the "what does the world actually do" gap that the operator pushed back on.

The four-promise test is non-negotiable. Single-line copy can elide one promise if the section copy reinforces it; the hero headline cannot elide two.

## The five patterns that solve the four-promise problem

These are the patterns that appear across at least two of the three survey threads.

### Pattern A — Three-verb premise (PvP board games)

**Sources (verbatim):**
- Catan Universe (catanuniverse.com): *"Trade – Build – Settle."*
- Cultist Simulator (weatherfactory.biz/cultist-simulator): *"Seize forbidden treasures. Summon alien gods. Feed on your disciples."*
- Cultist Simulator (loop): *"Find abominable rites in dreams. Craft tools and summon spirits. Indoctrinate innocents."*
- Dire Wolf Digital on Root: *"A game of adventure and war where players battle for control of a vast wilderness."*

**What it solves:** Promise 4 (feels like a game). Three concrete verbs anchor the loop without leaning on "infinite possibilities" hedge.
**What it doesn't solve:** Promises 1 (anything) and 2 (works) — the verb list implies a bounded loop, not unbounded agency.

### Pattern B — Verb catalogue premise (RimWorld, Caves of Qud)

**Sources (verbatim):**
- RimWorld (rimworldgame.com): *"You begin with three survivors of a shipwreck on a distant world. Manage colonists' moods, needs, wounds, illnesses and addictions. Build in the forest, desert, jungle, tundra, and more. Watch colonists develop and break relationships with family members, lovers, and spouses. Replace wounded limbs and organs with prosthetics, bionics, or biological parts harvested from others. Fight pirates, tribes, mad animals, giant insects and ancient killing machines."*
- Caves of Qud (cavesofqud.com): *"Do anything you can imagine. Dig a tunnel anywhere in the world. Purchase rare books from an albino ape mayor. Contract a fungal infection and grow glowing mushrooms on your hands. Charm a goat into joining you, then give him chain mail and a shotgun to equip. Clone yourself, mind-control your clone, hack off your own limbs, then eat them for sustenance."*

**What it solves:** ALL FOUR PROMISES at once. The catalogue IS the possibility promise AND the rule-bound game, because every verb is grounded in a specific game effect. This is the strongest single pattern for the proof-clip section.

**For Autogenesis body copy:** each proof-clip turn becomes one verb phrase lifted from `~/.tpipe/debug/trace/Round_*_Turn_*` data. *"Burn a forest. Annex a cult. Train a dolphin diplomat. Demolish the sky."* This is exactly what `references/consequence-loop-examples.md` documents in narrative form.

### Pattern C — Marriage framing (Slay the Spire, Balatro, Hades)

**Sources (verbatim):**
- Slay the Spire (store.steampowered.com/app/646570): *"We fused card games and roguelikes together to make the best single player deckbuilder we could."*
- Balatro (playbalatro.com): *"The poker roguelike."*
- Hades (IGN quote, supergiantgames.com): *"Hades is a one-of-a-kind rogue-lite that does a brilliant job of marrying its fast-paced action with its persistent, progressing story through a vividly reimagined Greek mythological underworld."*

**What it solves:** Promises 3 (fits in the rules) and 4 (feels like a game). By naming two rule-bound genres and promising the combination, the reader trusts the product is rule-bound (because one half always is).

**For Autogenesis hero:** this is the load-bearing frame. *"Narrative storytelling meets shared-history strategy."* *"Freeform action meets the rule-bound world."* Both halves must be real games so the "chatbot fear" is dispelled.

### Pattern D — Caves of Qud closed-form dual promise (THE SINGLE MOST USEFUL LINE)

**Source (verbatim):** *"The result is a hybrid handcrafted & procedurally-generated world where you can do just about anything."* — cavesofqud.com

**What it solves:** ALL FOUR PROMISES in one sentence. *"Hybrid handcrafted"* anchors craft, *"procedurally-generated"* anchors infinite possibility, *"you can do just about anything"* is unbounded agency with the *"just about"* hedge that keeps it rule-bound.

**For Autogenesis:** this is the test to hold any candidate premise against. Does it name the rule-bound anchor? The possibility anchor? Land the "just about anything" promise?

### Pattern E — Consequence closer (Disco Elysium, Dead Cells, Caves of Qud)

**Sources (verbatim):**
- Disco Elysium (store.steampowered.com/app/632470): *"Become a hero or an absolute disaster of a human being."*
- Dead Cells (deadcells.com): *"Kill, die, learn, repeat."*
- Vampire Survivors (poncle.games): *"The supernatural indie phenomenon that lets you be the bullet hell!"*
- Caves of Qud: *"You might be destroyed by inner demons, or torn apart by the red powers of the night, or become the herald of a new age."*

**What it solves:** Promise 3 (fits in the rules). The reader sees what winning and losing look like without needing the rules explained.

**For Autogenesis:** the proof-clip section closes with a consequence line per turn. The hero can close with a one-line "what happens" phrase naming both possible outcomes.

- Adaptive mastery: "Use your wits, your reflexes, and the items available to you" (Spelunky); "Kill, die, learn, repeat" (Dead Cells).

## Anti-patterns banned across the survey

- **"Powered by AI" as the hook** (AI Dungeon anti-pattern). The reader classifies this as ChatGPT-with-prompts.
- **"Cozy management game about dying"** (Spiritfarer tone). Too soft.
- **"UNCONVENTIONAL ADVENTURE RPG in an inexpressible world"** (Norco). Hedge-stacking.
- **"Beautiful, fun, memorable"** (Stonemaier homepage). Three adjectives, zero verbs.
- **"Innovative, immersive, unique, groundbreaking"** — universal ban; already in the Autogenesis skill catalog.
- **"Endless replayability"** without a concrete system that earns it (Dead Cells implicit rejection — they say "Kill, die, learn, repeat" instead).

## What to delete from existing Autogenesis landing-page drafts

These are patterns the survey confirmed as weak:

- *"Autogenesis is a PvP board game with no menu of moves"* — category-description opener. PvP board-game category pages (Dire Wolf, Stonemaier, Leder) DON'T open this way; they use three-verb tag loops or three-word promise stacks.
- *"Each turn you invent the action. The game turns it into a story, a judgment, and a consequence on the board."* — buried in clause 4 of a 4-clause sentence; lands as recap, not hook.
- *"The game starts with a character sheet. The rest is hidden."* — "hidden" implies the game has secrets. The truth is the world remembers everything in the open, the UI just renders little. Survey-confirmed: real game copy never leads with "hidden values."
- *"AWS Premier Partner, Co-sell · Clazar, AI Whitepapers · Avahi, Open-source Benchmarks"* trust pills — corporate boilerplate that belongs on the company page, not the game landing.

## Pipeline order: what to write first (validated against the corpus)

1. **Hero** — premise sentence + sub-line + hero reel (3 clips: typing → narrative → world-update).
2. **Verb catalogue** (NEW) — 8-12 real Autogenesis turns rendered as one-line verb phrases, lifted from `~/.tpipe/debug/trace/Round_*_Turn_*` data. This is the load-bearing change from the survey.
3. **Delayed-consequence loop** — operator's verbatim quote + king-of-England / revenge-of-deposed-leader examples.
4. **What a turn is** — five-state pipeline, reframed from "the player writes the input" to "the world tries to fit it inside the rules."
5. **Commander creation** — character sheet (Lord Maple Tree + Officer Dave + Zuzusarogorata + Talya).
6. **Studio band** — game-eating-its-own-benchmark.

Sections 2 and 3 are where the new premise sentence lives. The hero opens the door. Sections 2 and 3 close it.
