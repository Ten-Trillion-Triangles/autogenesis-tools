# Autogenesis Marketing — premise, voice, asset selection

## When to Load

Load this skill when ANY of the following are in play:

- Authoring, editing, or reviewing the Autogenesis landing page (hero copy, hero section, section order, CTA, demo reel placement)
- Writing marketing copy, comparison pages, blog posts, or social copy for Autogenesis
- Selecting or editing demo assets (GIFs, MP4, WebM, screenshots) from `public/gifs-final/`, `public/assets/blog/`, or trace-driven captures
- Designing hero composition, layout, or visual rhythm for the landing page
- Positioning Autogenesis against other products (TPipeWriter, AI Dungeon, AI Dungeon Master, etc.)
- Auditing hedge phrases, AI tells, or factual claims in Autogenesis marketing copy before ship
- Working on the "what is Autogenesis?" premise phrasing — the single hardest sentence in the product

## What Autogenesis IS — the load-bearing premise

The premise has to hold **four promises together** or it collapses into one of three failure modes ("AI writes funny stories", "strategy game with a chat box", OR the "what does the world actually do" gap where the loop is named but the response isn't). The four promises, lifted verbatim from the operator's 2026-08-09 framing — *"the concept that you can do ANYTHING and it works and still fits in the rules of the game and feels like a game is important"*:

1. **ANYTHING (unbounded agency).** Players are not selecting from a menu of legal actions. They invent an action in natural language and submit it. The validator gates whether the *play* is structurally legal under the 5-rule gate (no explicit outcomes, no new tech without prior mention, no controlling other players, no resource invention, no anti-retcon). It does NOT gate whether the action is "realistic" or "in-genre." Operator's verbatim: *"Even the limits is your own imagination. Even that's only partly true because very clever players can rewrite the rules of the world itself if they become powerful enough or clever enough to pull even that off."*
2. **WORKS (real response, not empty).** The world produces a real response — narrative prose, a judge verdict, territory changes. The reader must see that the world answers back. (Earlier framings collapsed this with promise 3; the operator's "and it works" makes it distinct.)
3. **FITS IN THE RULES (mechanical, not vibes).** The storywriter + judge pipeline turns the invented action into narrative prose, the judge decides success/failure and stat/territory changes, the world state updates. The result is mechanical — capture, depose, stat buffs/debuffs, NPC nemesis spawn, Elder God intervention — not a vibes-only response. Operator's verbatim: *"with each action being taken having an unpredicable result that creates a brand new experience that could never happen in any other setting of gameplay or narrative fiction."* This is the promise that distinguishes Autogenesis from AI Dungeon.
4. **FEELS LIKE A GAME (scoring, factions, win conditions visible).** Scoring, factions, win conditions (51% / 55% / 60% by player count), per-round stat decay, the validator/judge pipeline, the karma/nemesis system, the NPC cascade, the Elder God intervention — the rule layer is hardcoded in `server/.../gameplayOrchestrator.kt`, `TurnHarness.kt`, `judge.kt`, `validator.kt`. The product is a four-player board game with a hard 25-round cap and a 51/55/60% territory victory threshold. Operator's verbatim: *"It felt like less like a game more like an absurd collaborative story writing experience. Like a board game version of telephone."*

**Why four and not three:** the operator pushed back (2026-08-09) on the previous framing of promises 2+3 collapsed together ("the rules still apply and somehow it just works"). The verbatim phrasing splits them: "works" = the response exists; "fits in the rules" = the response is rule-bound. A premise that says "the world writes a story about your action" satisfies "works" but fails "fits in the rules" — that's the AI Dungeon failure mode. Single-line copy can elide one promise if the section copy reinforces it; the hero headline cannot elide two.

**Why this matters for premise-sentence selection:** Variant 1 (*"Invent any action. The world answers. The board changes."*) satisfies 1+2+3 but only *implies* 4. The operator's rejection (2026-08-09) was specifically: *"it doesn't really sell the concept as strong as it could be. Because the concept that you can do ANYTHING and it works and still fits in the rules of the game and feels like a game is important."* Any headline that does not name the rule-bound consequence explicitly (territory / win threshold / 25-round cap / four-player / board game vocabulary) collapses into the gap the operator identified. Pair the headline with section copy that names promise 4 — or rewrite the headline.

The further promise, unique to Autogenesis: even the rules of the world can become a move. A sufficiently clever or powerful player can rewrite the laws of physics themselves (e.g. Robert the Destroyer's demolition robots demolishing the sky). The skill at `autogenesis-game-mechanics` § Karma & Nemesis Spawn documents how karma accumulates and how the world's memory weaponizes itself.

## What Autogenesis IS NOT (anti-premises)

- **NOT an AI story generator.** The story is not the product; the story is the resolution channel. The product is the board game underneath it.
- **NOT a sandbox / open-world RPG.** The world has rules, point costs (50/50/50/1 per action type), per-round stat decay, and a hard cap of 25 rounds. Players don't "explore" — they compete.
- **NOT ChatGPT-with-a-DM-prompt.** The validator/rectifier/judge pipeline, the karma/nemesis system, the NPC cascade, the Elder God intervention, and the win thresholds (51% / 55% / 60% by player count) are all hardcoded in `server/.../gameplayOrchestrator.kt`, `TurnHarness.kt`, `judge.kt`, `validator.kt`. The LLM is the storyteller, not the rules.
- **NOT "AI Dungeon + world map."** The history of the table-test version matters: this began as a D&D-like experience with NovelAI as the story writer and humans managing rules by hand. Automation moved rules management into the game, but player interaction became character-to-character action inside the shared narrative — there is no out-of-character chat between players.
- **NOT a hidden-information game.** All world history is shared between every player. The UI intentionally renders very little, but the Answer Agent exposes the hidden values on request. No fog of war. The uncertainty is in what a fact will eventually mean and how another player or NPC will exploit it, not in what is visible.

## The "premise sentence" problem

This is the hardest single sentence in the product. It has to:

- Survive a reader who skims in three seconds.
- Avoid "X is not Y, X is Z" copula avoidance (the humanizer pass bans it for Bigwang voice; the principle generalizes).
- Land **all four** load-bearing promises: any action can be attempted, the world produces a real response, the response fits inside the rules, the result feels like a real game.
- Avoid reducing the game to "AI writes funny stories" or "strategy game with a chat box."
- Land the emotional hit (excitement → anticipation → being blown away) before any copy explains the mechanics.

A landing-page premise that works will usually lead with the loop in action, not with adjectives. The four canonical loop variants that have worked in drafts and transcripts:

- "Invent any action. The world answers. The board changes."
- "Type what you do. The world decides what happens. The board records it."
- "You can do anything. The game decides if it worked. The board shows the cost."
- "Any move you can imagine, in a world that answers back, judged by rules that make it mean something."

**Status flag (2026-08-09):** the operator pushed back on Variant 1 specifically, noting that "it doesn't really sell the concept as strong as it could be. Because the concept that you can do ANYTHING and it works and still fits in the rules of the game and feels like a game is important." Variant 1 satisfies promises 1+2 but elides the "fits in the rules" anchor (promise 3) and the "feels like a game" anchor (promise 4) — the territory/board/pieces language implies a board game, but the *rule-bound consequence* (territory counts, scoring, 51/55/60% victory thresholds, 25-round hard cap) isn't named explicitly. Treat all four variants as **drafts, not final answers**. Any of them can be the final headline only after the section copy reinforces promise 3 and promise 4.

The operator's own phrasing from interviews is the load-bearing source. Capture the operator's words verbatim and lift, don't paraphrase. Paraphrase loses the inflection that made the operator's version hit.

**External grounding is mandatory before recommending a premise sentence** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). The four canonical variants above were generated from internal paraphrase. The operator's request: *"Why don't we conceptually go find a bunch of landing pages for pvp games and similar game concepts and see what those look like. Learn the wording more."* Before recommending ANY premise sentence as final, survey landing pages of direct PvP board games (Twilight Imperium, Root, Gloomhaven, etc.), AI-driven narrative games (Hidden Door, Inworld, Hades, Disco Elysium — treat AI Dungeon as an anti-pattern), and roguelikes / possibility-driven games (Balatro, Slay the Spire, Cultist Simulator, etc.). Capture verbatim premise sentences and identify the "build your own X" pattern most directly applicable to Autogenesis's "invent your own action" framing. See `references/external-game-survey.md` for the canonical survey template and `references/peer-game-pitch-survey-2026-08-09.md` for the verified findings from the 2026-08-09 survey (five patterns, seven candidate premises, anti-pattern list).

The "delayed consequence" loop — the king-of-England-nukes-Canada example, the revenge-of-the-deposed-leader example — is real but lives at the turn-history level, not as a single GIF. ## Voice & register

The product itself is loud, absurd, and violent. The marketing copy does NOT have to match that register; it has to transmit the *fact* of that register without being a wall of noise.

Three voice axes that work for Autogenesis marketing:

- **Bigwang swagger** (load `persona:bigwang` skill) — for the operator's byline or for hero copy that has a face attached. Use sparingly; the Bigwang pitfalls around closers, AI-isms, and copula avoidance still apply. Audit before ship.
- **Direct declarative** — for the premise sentence and the section headers. No "we think," no "honest assessment," no soft superlatives. State what the product is, what it does, what it isn't.
- **Concrete / document-the-evidence** — for the proof clips and the post-mortem material. Quote actual turns. Name actual players. Show actual judge verdicts. The product itself produces the copy; the agent's job is to lift, not embellish.

Hedge phrases to ban on Autogenesis marketing surfaces: `honest assessment`, `reasonable choice`, `well-suited`, `well-architected`, `vibrant community`, `extensive documentation`, `an architectural ceiling`, `Not sure which comparison`, `stay with LangGraph`, `it could be argued`, `perhaps`. Grep before ship.

## What the landing page MUST do (in order)

1. **Open with the premise.** Loop in three beats, no setup. The reader should know what the game is before they hit the fold.
2. **Show the loop in motion.** Hero reel = the 5 GIF sequence, with the typing → narrative → world-update beats as the focal points.
3. **Prove it works.** Quote 1-2 real turns (e.g. Lord Maple Tree's microscopic vascular war or Officer Dave's Criminality Indices). Trace → judge verdict → territory change is the proof shape.
4. **Name the consequence loop.** "What you do comes back. Sometimes you see it coming. Sometimes you don't." Third section.
5. **Close with the design philosophy, not a pitch.** No "is the right answer for teams" closers. End on a third-person observation about the product or the world it creates.

## What the landing page MUST NOT do

- "X is not Y, X is Z" copula avoidance in any registered voice. State what X IS, period.
- Lead with "AI-powered" or "AI dungeon master" before showing the loop. The reader who sees those words first decides this is a ChatGPT-with-prompts game. Show the loop first.
- Show the LLM streaming the work stream (`02-agent-stream.gif`) as the hero focal point. The agent work stream is what scares engineers and bores everyone else. Use it as background b-roll.
- Lead with a screenshot of the top bar (Main Score / Military / Diplomatic / Research / Summit) without context. Numbers without a story are not a hook.
- Hide the four-player / 25-round / competitive nature of the game behind the story. The story is the resolution channel; the product is the four-player board game underneath. Both must surface.

## Anti-patterns observed in past drafts

- Opening with "Autogenesis is a unique AI-driven strategy game..." — too soft, too many words, no loop. Replace with the three-beat premise.
- Hero reel as a single GIF that loops the typing input forever — static, doesn't show resolution. Use the 5-GIF sequence.
- "Features" section listing Mechanical / Diplomatic / Research / Summit play types as bullet points without example turns. The features list is true but un-evocative. Lift example turns instead.
- Comparison table vs LangChain / AutoGen / CrewAI. Autogenesis isn't in that category and the comparison makes the reader think it is. If a comparison page exists (per AGENTS.md audit rules), keep the focus on Autogenesis's actual category: "the AI-dungeon-master-driven 4-player board game with no menu of legal actions."
- Closing with "if you're evaluating X for Y, here's what to look for" / "if you're a fan of Z, you'll love Autogenesis." The closer is observed, not pitched.
- **Recommending a premise sentence without external-game-survey grounding** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). Internal paraphrase of the operator's interview produces variants that are technically true but commercially weak. Before promoting any premise sentence to "final," survey landing pages of direct PvP board games, AI-driven narrative games, and roguelike / possibility-driven games, and lift the actual wording patterns that move product. See `references/external-game-survey.md` and the verified findings in `references/peer-game-pitch-survey-2026-08-09.md`.
- **Loop-only premise that omits the "works + fits in the rules + feels like a game" anchors** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). Variant 1 (*"Invent any action. The world answers. The board changes."*) was rejected for eliding promises 2-4 (or promises 3-4 if you collapse 2+3 as the previous framing did). The four-promise standard is the test. Any headline that satisfies 1+2 but elides 3+4 will collapse into the "AI writes funny stories" or "AI chatbot" failure mode. Pair the headline with section copy (proof clips, validator/judge pipeline, win threshold numbers) that names the rule-bound consequence explicitly.
- **Describe-the-game vs describe-the-player — the load-bearing distinction for premise sentences** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). Premise sentences that describe the game (third-person: "the world writes the story," "the validator checks the play") fail commercially. Premise sentences that describe what the PLAYER does (imperative: "create your commander," "take any action") land. Test any future premise sentence against this distinction FIRST. The locked hero copy (operator-confirmed 2026-08-09) is describe-the-player; the four canonical variants were all describe-the-game. Worked example (canonical Section 3 opener, operator-supplied 2026-08-12): *"Whenever characters in the story become relevant enough, they spring to life and take their own turns."* The verbatim line is from the operator's interview transcript, NOT rewritten by the assistant. It opens the section in the operator's voice. The body paragraphs that follow expand the verbatim meaning but do NOT paraphrase the verbatim itself. Pattern: **section opener = operator-verbatim transcript quote** when the operator has said it before in their own voice. Body paragraphs = chief strategist's expansion of the verbatim meaning. Don't blur the distinction — verbatim stays verbatim, body expands the meaning.
- **Numbers don't sell in the hero closer** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). *"Four players. Twenty-five rounds. One shared world. No two games are ever the same."* was rejected as weak. Three numbers and an abstraction don't tell the reader what happens. The closer should answer the implied question (*what happens after I write the move?*), not restate game structure. Numbers move into the body sections where the rule-bound game language belongs. The hero closer does ONE thing.
- **Operator cadence works in the hero but breaks down across a full page** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). The locked hero copy uses the operator's interview-transcript cadence verbatim: *"Every game has rules. In most games, the rules will limit what you can do... But not here. Here, the limit is your own imagination."* That cadence lands as the front door. Trying to do the entire page in that cadence (Draft F, 2026-08-09) failed — operator verdict: *"Everything up to here in F is strong. This sounds human. This sounds correct. After that it falls apart."* The body uses the four-beat rhetorical move (establish common ground → name constraint → pivot → promise) WITHOUT trying to mimic operator single-sentence declarations. Imperative verbs throughout, but structured paragraphs.
- **Parallel negation leaks into every draft in a draft set, not just one** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). The body of Section 3 was drafted as three parallel options (A2, A3, A4). All three drafts shipped parallel-negation closers — *"You are not playing against a board. You are playing against a world that has taken an interest in what happens next"* — and parallel-negation body phrases (*"are not the NPCs you end up dealing with," "are not the grudges that come back"*). The agent drafted one closer, then copy-pasted the same closer into the other two drafts, then forgot the rule applied to all of them. The audit must run against the WHOLE DRAFT SET, not per-sentence review. Operator verdict: *"All of that has too many negative negation. Not x its y patterns ... ie parallel negation."* Detection: `grep -ciE 'is not|isn.t|didn.t|won.t|aren.t|wasn.t|weren.t|don.t|doesn.t|can.t|but not|no longer|not just|not only' src/content/blog/<post>.md` (body only) returns 0 in the hero AND every body section that has its own closer. The pattern recurs across the page; the audit must recurse across the page. Closers that mirror a previously-approved structure (the hero's "But not here / Here Y" pivot) are the highest-risk site — if the agent is tempted to reuse the hero's pivot shape in a body closer, the safer move is a fresh third-person observation, NOT a parallel structure with a different noun pair.
- **Premise sentences must name goal + opponents + stake, not just the loop** (operator correction, 2026-08-09). The four canonical variants named the loop (invent / world answers / board changes) but didn't name WHY the player plays. The operator-supplied hero sub (*"Create your commander anyway you like. Take any action you can think of to expand your empire and dominate all your rival empires for control of the world."*) names all four: complete control ("anyway you like"), unbounded agency ("any action you can think of"), a goal ("expand your empire"), opponents ("your rival empires"), a stake ("control of the world"). Any new premise sentence must name at least goal + opponents + stake — loop alone is insufficient.

- **Paired-promise headline pattern (canonical Section 5 headline, operator-approved 2026-08-12)** — a body-section headline can name two of the four promises in one sentence if the second promise is the *resolution* of the first. The canonical Section 5 headline: *"Anything you can imagine. The game builds around it."* The first clause names the imagination promise (anything you can imagine). The second clause names the structural-coherence promise — the game handles whatever the player imagines. The reader sees: imagination promise → game-handles-it promise, in two sentences. The pattern works because the second clause is the *answer* to the implied question from the first clause ("what happens when I imagine it?"). The body paragraph that follows names the cause-and-effect clause ("Burn a forest and the timber is gone. Annex a cult and the temples change allegiance."). Test for any paired-promise headline: (a) the second clause must be the direct answer to the first clause's implied question, (b) both promises must be operator-confirmed, (c) the body paragraph that follows must name at least one of the four promises that didn't make the headline. Worked example from the v10 canonical doc: Section 5 headline = *"Anything you can imagine. The game builds around it."* → body para 1 names imagination promise → body para 2 names game-handles-it promise → body para 3 names cause-and-effect promise. All four promises land across headline + body.

- **Ship a single canonical doc with status-per-section from the START, not a sprawl of unsynchronized drafts** (workflow pitfall, 2026-08-09 → 2026-08-12). The Section 4 / Section 5 work on the Autogenesis landing page generated 12+ draft files (`autogenesis-drafts-v2.md`, `-v3.md`, `-draft-v4.md`, `-v5.md`, `-v6-section3.md`, `-v7-section3-livingworld.md`, `-v8-section3-livingworld.md`, `-v9-section3-livingworld.md`, `-section3-final-A4.md`, `-section5-premise-pitch-v2.md`, `-section5-what-a-turn-is-pitch.md`, `-doc-audit.md`) before the operator asked for a separate audit pass to consolidate them. The operator's reaction: *"Ok lets go through all the docs so far and verify if thgey are matchinng what twe decided here in this chat so far."* This is the same failure mode as the multi-message consultation pattern — the assistant produces drafts in linear sequence and the operator has to reconstruct state. **Default to: produce a canonical file at the START of any multi-section landing-page work, with one section per heading and a status row per section (LOCKED / DRAFT / SUPERSEDED / TBD). Add new sections to the canonical file as drafts arrive. Mark superseded drafts in their first line with `> **SUPERSEDED.** Canonical: <path>` so the sprawl doesn't fragment the canonical source of truth.** Working on more than ~3 section drafts in any one landing-page session without a canonical file accumulating drift = the canonical file needs to exist.

- **Per-section reframe happens on operator initiative, not on assistant initiative** (workflow pattern, 2026-08-12). Section 4 ("what a turn is") and Section 5 ("imagination + cause-and-effect") were both reframed by the operator through separate pitch docs (`autogenesis-section5-what-a-turn-is-pitch.md` and `autogenesis-section5-premise-pitch-v2.md`) AFTER the v4/v5 cycle had already locked "No two games are alike" as Section 5. The assistant did not flag the duplication risk; the operator did. When the operator creates a new pitch doc with a different framing, **treat that pitch as a REPLACEMENT for the prior section, not as a parallel option to choose between**. The operator creates new docs to OVERRIDE prior decisions, not to add to them. Check the file's header section ("v1 was rejected because…", "operator caught X", "operator is reframing") for the directional signal before deciding whether the new file is a replacement or a parallel option. When in doubt: assume REPLACEMENT, update the canonical doc, mark the prior doc SUPERSEDED. The decision to add parallel options is the operator's call, not the assistant's.

Closers that mirror a previously-approved structure (the hero's "But not here / Here Y" pivot, the v8 closer, the four-promise headline structure) are the highest-risk site — if the assistant is tempted to reuse a structural pattern in a body section, the safer move is a fresh third-person observation, NOT a parallel structure with a different noun pair.

Body sections can use a variant of the structure but must NOT use the same "Most games X / But not here / Here Y" pivot as the hero — the reader's eye treats repeated structure as visual repetition. Acceptable body alternatives: scene-led opener, single declarative, operator-verbatim quote as opener, four-clause direct statement (Section 3 Draft 3-NPC-A2 pattern). The "But not here" pivot is a hero-only move.**

The body's job is the proof. Appending qualifications to a locked punch line — even from the operator's own transcript — dilutes the land.
